ZIF-8 and cancer
Nanomedicine, without the jargon

A zinc crystal that only opens in the tumor.

ZIF-8 is a material made of zinc and an organic molecule, riddled with millions of microscopic pores. Researchers load medicine inside it. In an acidic environment, like a tumor's, the crystal breaks apart and releases its cargo.

The problem ZIF-8 tries to solve

1

Medicine that hits everything

Classic chemotherapy drugs travel through the whole body and harm healthy cells. That causes nausea, hair loss, and damage to the heart and kidneys.

2

Medicine that won't dissolve

Many promising compounds, like curcumin, dissolve poorly in water and degrade before reaching the tumor.

3

Tumors that resist

Tumor cells learn to expel the drug or to protect themselves. Delivering the cargo into the cell all at once helps overcome this barrier.

The idea

Pack the medicine into a particle thousands of times thinner than a hair, one that stays closed in blood and opens where the environment is acidic. The tumor and the inside of cells are acidic. The rest of the body is not.

Structure

Inside ZIF-8

The formula is simple: one zinc and two imidazolates, Zn(2-methylimidazolate)₂. The way the pieces fit creates empty cages, and that is where the cargo is stored.

drag to rotate

The sodalite cage

Each cage has 24 zinc atoms at its vertices and 36 imidazolate bridges along its edges, like an octahedron with cut corners. The six square faces and eight hexagonal faces make up the wall. The windows are in the hexagonal faces.

Why does it look like a zeolite?

The Zn–imidazolate–Zn angle is similar to the Si–O–Si angle in zeolites, porous minerals used in industry. That is why the name includes "zeolitic." The topology of ZIF-8 is called sodalite.

The whole crystal

Millions of these cages, stacked together, form a particle shaped like a rhombic dodecahedron, with 12 rhombus-shaped faces. That is the figure on the home page.

The basic unit

A zinc in the center and four imidazolates around it. Each ring connects two zinc atoms like a bridge. Click on the parts.

Other ZIFs

ZIF-8

Zinc + 2-methylimidazole. The most studied in medicine because it is easy to make in water and opens in acidic conditions.

ZIF-67

Cobalt instead of zinc, same topology. Cobalt is chemically active, which is why it appears in catalytic therapies.

ZIF-90

Uses imidazole-2-carboxaldehyde, which has a reactive aldehyde group. It lets molecules be attached to the framework itself.

Engineering

How to make and load it

ZIF-8 forms in water, at room temperature, in minutes. That is what makes it possible to build it already with the drug inside.

Basic recipe

A zinc salt (nitrate or acetate) and 2-methylimidazole, in water or methanol. Mix and stir for a few minutes.

Size control

The ratio of ligand to zinc, the solvent, the temperature, and additives change the size, from tens of nanometers to micrometers. For blood use, the target is usually below a few hundred nanometers.

Green and simple

No heat or harsh solvents, which protects fragile molecules like proteins, enzymes, and RNA.

Three ways to load the cargo

A smart surface

Pure ZIF-8 has a positive charge and is recognized by the body. Coatings add stealth, targeting, and identity. Choose a layer.

How to tell it worked

XRD: is it really ZIF-8?

X-ray diffraction gives a peak pattern characteristic of the sodalite structure. If the peaks match ZIF-8, the crystal formed.

SEM and TEM: shape and size

Electron microscopy shows the rhombic dodecahedron shape and the size of each particle, as well as coating layers.

DLS: size in water

Light scattering measures the size of the particle swollen in liquid, which is larger than what is seen under the microscope.

Zeta potential: surface charge

Shows whether the surface is positive or negative. Pure ZIF-8 tends to be positive, and coatings change that value.

BET: area and pores

Nitrogen adsorption measures surface area and pore volume. It drops when the drug fills the space, which helps prove the drug got in.

UV-vis and HPLC: how much drug went in

They measure the amount of drug left in solution. By difference, you calculate how much was loaded.

What the cargo does

Eight ways to attack

ZIF-8 carries the weapon to the tumor. Choose a strategy and activate the scene.

Combining is the rule

Recent studies combine several mechanisms in a single particle. A 2026 example uses engineered bacteria coated with ZIF-8, loaded with a photosensitizer and catalase, that release interleukin-2 and even trigger pyroptosis, combined with PD-L1 blockade.

Chemo + light

The drug attacks DNA while heat or radicals weaken the cell. A gold nanostar in a ZIF-8 shell is a classic example.

Zinc + drug

The released zinc can reverse drug resistance, as shown in a study with gefitinib in lung cancer.

Light + immunity

Killing cells in a way that alerts the immune system can turn a "cold" tumor into one the body recognizes.

Targets and testing

Which cancers

Laboratories test ZIF-8 in cultured tumor cells, called cell lines, and then in mice. Each cancer type poses a different challenge.

What to look for in ZIF-8 results
  • How much drug was loaded (loading capacity) and how much was retained (efficiency).
  • Release at pH 7.4 and at acidic pH.
  • Cell tests and, if any, animal tests.
  • Particle size and toxicity of the empty material.
  • Whether there is a control group with the free drug.

Questions and quiz

Is ZIF-8 already used in patients?

The studies are laboratory and animal studies. It is still in preclinical research and is not an approved medicine.

Is zinc harmful?

The body needs zinc, but excess can be toxic. Some studies use the released zinc as part of the attack, while others measure safety in organs and blood. The dose and fate of the zinc are points of attention.

Why does the crystal open in acid?

Protons compete with zinc for the nitrogen of the imidazole and the bond breaks. The network falls apart on its own, with no need for a sensor.

What is the difference from a liposome?

Liposomes are fat bubbles, used in approved medicines. ZIF-8 is a rigid crystal with high loading capacity and pH-sensitive release, but with less safety history.

Why doesn't blood open the crystal?

At pH 7.4 the network is stable. But phosphate in the blood can slowly degrade ZIF-8, so coatings are used to buy time.

Can you say it cures cancer?

No. The results show potential in laboratory models. Moving to people requires safety studies, large-scale manufacturing, and clinical trials.

What is still a challenge?
  • Stability in blood.
  • Long-term toxicity and the fate of zinc.
  • Producing the same particles every time, at scale.
  • Comparing studies done with different methods.
  • Proving efficacy and safety in humans.
